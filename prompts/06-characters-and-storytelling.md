# Characters, Comics, and Storyboards

## 1. Original character anchor sheet

**Best for:** building a consistent series · **Format:** 3:2 · **Difficulty:** Advanced

```text
Create an original character anchor sheet for {{character_role}} named {{name}}. Show front, three-quarter,
profile, back, full-body neutral pose, four expressions, hand detail, signature prop, outfit construction,
and six color swatches. Traits: {{physical_traits}}. Personality visible through design: {{traits}}.
Medium: {{medium}} on a clean neutral sheet.

Keep facial geometry, proportions, costume seams, and palette identical across every view. No existing IP,
celebrity likeness, named living-artist imitation, text except supplied labels, or alternate costumes.
```

## 2. Four-panel silent comic

**Best for:** short social stories · **Format:** 4:5 · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

```text
Image 1: approved character anchor. Create a vertical four-panel silent comic about {{story_premise}}.
Panel beats: 1 {{setup}}, 2 {{complication}}, 3 {{turn}}, 4 {{payoff}}. Use clear changes in shot size and
eye line while preserving the character's face, proportions, outfit, and palette. Style: {{style_properties}}.

Exactly four equal panels, no speech bubbles, captions, symbols, logos, or extra characters. Each panel must
show one readable action and the sequence must work without text.
```

## 3. Film storyboard contact sheet

**Best for:** previsualization · **Format:** 16:9 · **Difficulty:** Advanced

```text
Create a black-and-white storyboard contact sheet for {{scene}} with exactly six numbered frames:
1 establishing wide, 2 medium action, 3 insert detail, 4 reaction close-up, 5 moving point-of-view,
6 final reveal. Add simple arrows only for camera or subject movement. Under each frame render shot number,
shot size, and {{short_action_note}}.

Maintain screen direction, character wardrobe, prop continuity, time of day, and location geometry. No polished
color art, extra frames, dialogue, or impossible camera placement.
```

## 4. Children's-book double-page spread

**Best for:** picture-book development · **Format:** 2:1 · **Difficulty:** Advanced

```text
Images 1–2: approved character and environment anchors. Create a 2:1 double-page spread where {{character}}
{{action}} in {{setting}}. Preserve anchor identity and visual language. Compose a clear left-to-right story
path with the gutter kept free of faces, hands, and essential objects. Leave a calm text area of {{location}}
for later typesetting. Mood: {{mood}}; medium: {{medium_properties}}.

No text, page numbers, existing storybook characters, or extra plot events. Keep the action age-appropriate
and the character design unchanged.
```

## 5. Character-consistent scene continuation

**Best for:** episodic illustration · **Format:** 3:2 · **Difficulty:** Advanced

```text
Image 1: approved character anchor. Image 2: most recent approved scene for continuity.
Continue the story in a new 3:2 scene: {{new_action_and_location}}. Preserve facial structure, apparent age,
hair, proportions, outfit construction, signature prop, palette, and medium. Carry forward only these continuity
details from Image 2: {{continuity_details}}. Change camera to {{new_camera}} and mood to {{new_mood}}.

Do not redesign the character, repeat the previous composition, add text, or introduce unrequested characters.
Verify identity against Image 1, not against any imperfect intermediate frame.
```
