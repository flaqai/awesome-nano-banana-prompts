# History, Culture, and Heritage Prompts

## 1. Period-accurate street scene

**Best for:** museums, education, and documentary concepts · **Format:** 3:2 · **Difficulty:** Advanced

```text
Create a historically responsible 3:2 street scene set in {{place}} during {{specific_year_or_period}}.
Show {{verified_everyday_activity}} with architecture, paving, transport, tools, signage, clothing layers,
hairstyles, lighting sources, and social behavior supported by {{approved_sources}}. Camera: human eye level;
style: naturalistic documentary reconstruction, not heroic spectacle.

Do not combine eras, modernize cleanliness, invent uniforms or text, add famous people, or turn cultural details
into fantasy decoration. If a detail is uncertain, choose a visually neutral omission and report it separately.
```

## 2. Museum exhibit interpretation panel

**Best for:** public-history design · **Format:** 16:9 · **Difficulty:** Advanced

```text
Design a 16:9 museum interpretation panel about {{artifact_or_topic}} for {{audience}}. Include one rights-cleared
hero image, one detail crop, a short timeline, and exactly {{label_count}} callouts. Render only the approved title,
intro, labels, date, provenance, and credit: {{approved_copy}}. Use accessible contrast and a calm reading order.

Do not invent provenance, ownership, date, quotation, restoration claim, cultural meaning, or donor credit.
Keep captions adjacent to the correct image and verify every proper noun and date.
```

## 3. Classical scroll-style narrative

**Best for:** original heritage-inspired storytelling · **Format:** 21:9 · **Difficulty:** Advanced

```text
Create an original 21:9 continuous-scroll narrative about {{verified_historical_or_fictional_event}} divided into
{{scene_count}} connected episodes. Use {{period_and_region}} material cues: paper or silk tone, perspective convention,
architecture, clothing, landscape rhythm, and restrained mineral-color palette. Repeat the main character only where
the continuous-narrative convention requires it, with consistent identity and costume.

Do not copy an existing scroll, imitate a named living artist, mix dynasties, invent calligraphy, or add seals.
If text is required, render only {{approved_inscription}} and verify script and historical appropriateness.
```

## 4. Artifact reconstruction board

**Best for:** archaeology and conservation communication · **Format:** 3:2 · **Difficulty:** Advanced

```text
Images 1–{{n}}: documented views of {{artifact}}. Create a reconstruction board with current condition, measured
fragment arrangement, a conservative reconstructed silhouette, material cross-section, and a clear visual distinction
between observed evidence and inferred completion. Use labels "OBSERVED", "INFERRED", and "UNKNOWN" exactly.

Do not hide damage, invent decoration, color, text, function, dimensions, or missing parts. Keep uncertainty visible,
cite the supplied evidence in accompanying text, and ensure inferred areas cannot be mistaken for original material.
```

## 5. Heritage craft process

**Best for:** education and cultural documentation · **Format:** 4:5 · **Difficulty:** Advanced

```text
Create a 4:5 process poster documenting {{craft}} in {{region_or_tradition}}. Show exactly {{step_count}} verified
stages, correct tools, materials, hand positions, workshop context, and maker safety practices based on {{sources}}.
Use respectful documentary illustration and render only the approved step names: {{step_labels}}.

Do not merge distinct traditions, romanticize unsafe practices, invent sacred meaning, costume the maker, or claim
authenticity without evidence. Verify tool-to-step mapping and request community review before publication.
```
