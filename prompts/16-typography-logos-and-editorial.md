# Typography, Logos, and Editorial Prompts

## 1. Multiscript type specimen poster

**Best for:** multilingual identity testing · **Format:** 2:3 · **Difficulty:** Advanced

```text
Create a 2:3 type specimen poster testing {{brand_voice}} across {{scripts_or_locales}}. Use one headline scale,
one subheading scale, one short body sample per script, numerals, punctuation, and a compact color key. Approved
copy only: {{verbatim_copy_by_locale}}. Establish optical rather than mechanical size matching between scripts.

Do not translate, add filler text, mix glyph variants, distort letterforms, or force identical line breaks.
Verify diacritics, shaping, punctuation, numerals, reading direction, and every character before finalizing.
```

## 2. Original logo exploration sheet

**Best for:** early identity development · **Format:** 3:2 · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

```text
Create an original 3:2 logo exploration sheet for the fictional brand "{{brand_name}}", which provides
{{service_or_product}} to {{audience}}. Explore exactly six distinct concepts derived from {{brand_idea}}, each
with a strong monochrome silhouette and short concept label. Include one wordmark direction and one symbol-only
direction. Keep geometry simple enough for small-size use.

No existing trademarks, letterform copies, app-icon mockups, gradients, 3D effects, slogans, or random symbols.
Render the brand name exactly and ensure all six concepts are materially different.
```

## 3. Editorial cover grid system

**Best for:** magazines and reports · **Format:** 3:4 · **Difficulty:** Advanced

```text
Design a 3:4 cover for the fictional publication "{{publication_name}}" using a disciplined modular grid.
Hero topic: {{topic}}; hero visual: {{original_visual_direction}}. Render exactly: masthead, "{{issue_line}}",
"{{main_cover_line}}", and {{supporting_cover_lines}}. Preserve a clear first, second, and third read.

No barcode, price, celebrity, fake contributor, copied publication style, decorative microtext, or extra cover line.
Keep the masthead legible and verify all copy, punctuation, and alignment.
```

## 4. Physical material lettering

**Best for:** campaign headlines and playful branding · **Format:** 16:9 · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

```text
Create the exact phrase "{{short_phrase}}" physically constructed from {{material}} inside {{environment}}.
Every letter must remain readable while obeying the material's weight, joints, texture, gravity, shadows, and wear.
Composition: {{composition}}; camera: {{camera}}; lighting: {{lighting}}. Use no additional written language.

Do not replace letters with unrelated objects, misspell or repeat words, create impossible unsupported forms,
or add a logo or watermark. Verify the phrase character by character and keep material scale consistent.
```

## 5. Wayfinding signage family

**Best for:** campuses, venues, and public spaces · **Format:** 16:9 · **Difficulty:** Advanced

```text
Create a 16:9 wayfinding family for {{venue_type}} showing one directory, one directional blade sign, one room
identifier, and one accessible amenity sign. Use the approved destinations, arrows, icons, numbering, and bilingual
copy: {{wayfinding_data}}. Maintain a consistent grid, icon weight, arrow system, contrast, and mounting logic.

Do not invent destinations, accessibility claims, emergency routes, regulatory symbols, or translations. Verify
arrow direction, room number, language pairing, legibility distance, and color contrast.
```
