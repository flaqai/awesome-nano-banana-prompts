# Profiles, Teams, and Lifestyle Prompts

## 1. Consistent professional headshot

**Best for:** profiles and speaker pages · **Format:** 4:5 · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

```text
Images 1–3: consenting subject identity references. Create a professional 4:5 head-and-shoulders portrait for
{{professional_context}}. Preserve face shape, apparent age, skin tone, hairline, hairstyle, eye color, distinguishing
features, and natural asymmetry. Wardrobe: {{approved_wardrobe}}; background: {{simple_background}}; light: soft,
credible studio setup; expression: {{expression}}.

Do not beautify, de-age, change body shape, whiten teeth or skin, add jewelry, imitate another person, or invent a logo.
Keep real skin texture and compare identity against all references.
```

## 2. Multi-person team portrait

**Best for:** company and community pages · **Format:** 16:9 · **Difficulty:** Advanced

```text
Images 1–{{person_count}}: consenting identity references. Create a natural 16:9 team portrait in {{work_environment}}.
Preserve each person's identity, age, skin tone, hair, body proportions, accessibility devices, and approved clothing.
Arrange varied heights and depths with natural posture and unobstructed faces. Use one camera, coherent focal plane,
shared color temperature, contact shadows, and environmental reflections.

Do not merge faces, duplicate people, exchange clothing, remove mobility aids, change body size, add staff, or place
someone in a tokenizing position. Count and verify every person independently.
```

## 3. Lifestyle persona scenario pack

**Best for:** service design and research communication · **Format:** 3:2 contact sheet · **Difficulty:** Advanced

```text
Create a three-scene contact sheet illustrating the fictional persona {{persona_description}} during {{workflow}}:
1 {{before_context}}, 2 {{core_action}}, 3 {{after_or_failure_state}}. Preserve the same original person, clothing,
device, environment logic, and time progression across all scenes. Use candid documentary photography and show the
real constraints {{constraints}} without turning them into visual stereotypes.

No real-person identity, brand logo, motivational caption, demographic cliché, perfect staged behavior, or UI text
that was not supplied. Exactly three scenes, each with one observable research insight.
```

## 4. Original avatar system

**Best for:** communities and app prototypes · **Format:** square grid · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

```text
Create a system of exactly {{avatar_count}} original avatars for {{community_or_app}}. Maintain one consistent crop,
background shape, line weight, rendering style, and lighting while varying face shapes, skin tones, ages, hair textures,
head coverings, glasses, and expressions respectfully. Every avatar must remain readable at 48 pixels.

No celebrity or real-person likeness, cultural costume shorthand, random disability symbolism, duplicated face,
text, logo, or hierarchy of detail. Keep representation varied without making identity the joke.
```

## 5. Remote-team editorial mosaic

**Best for:** distributed-team storytelling · **Format:** 16:9 · **Difficulty:** Advanced

```text
Images 1–{{person_count}}: consenting participant references and approved environments. Create a 16:9 editorial mosaic
with one equal-weight panel per participant, showing each person engaged in {{shared_activity}} from their real context.
Preserve identity, workspace details approved for publication, local light, and assistive equipment. Unify the mosaic
through crop rhythm, border system, and restrained color treatment rather than fabricating one shared room.

Do not merge locations, invent employer branding, alter screens, expose private data, add participants, or rank panels.
Verify consent scope, count, name spelling if captions are supplied, and removal of sensitive background information.
```
